# Texture surface painting

Texture paint is the workspace's default painting method. It writes blend weights into a linear texture instead of interpolating vertex colors. A small brush can paint the middle of an unsubdivided cube face. It brushes materials onto a surface; it does not place image stickers or Unity Decal Projectors.

## First stroke

1. Open **Window > SimpleBlend > Workspace**. Keep **Painting method > Texture paint** and select one active static mesh object.
2. Read the setup notice and choose **Start painting**. No palette or save dialog is required. SimpleBlend creates a uniquely named asset under `Assets/SimpleBlendPaint/`, selects the painted copy and activates the brush. Nothing is created merely by selecting an object or opening the workspace.
3. Setup creates a renderer-only sibling and hides the original renderer. It does not delete the original, move its children, copy scripts/colliders or migrate its vertex paint. The original collider remains where it was. The painted copy starts at Base. Without a palette, common source-material albedo/tint/normal inputs, tiling and basic scalar settings seed Base, with an editable overlay. Custom shader effects and differently packed PBR maps are not translated automatically.
4. Choose a surface and drag in the Scene view. Surface selection, Paint/Erase/Smooth, brush edits, Fill and presets keep the paint tool active. Changing configuration finishes the current stroke before the next one starts. Project asset selection keeps the bound paint object; selecting a different scene object, multiple objects or clearing selection stops the tool. Alt navigates; Escape stops painting. Focus loss finishes the stroke and releases the mouse, without deactivating the tool.
5. Try a radius of 0.1 m on a default one-metre cube. The ring is the projected outer radius. Falloff controls softness; Strength × Flow controls the maximum influence of a dab. **Capped per stroke** prevents overlapping dabs from repeatedly strengthening one stroke. **Build up along stroke** accumulates them. Existing brush preferences are preserved rather than silently reset.
6. Choose **Erase** to reduce the selected overlay toward Base while preserving competing overlay weights. Erasing Base reduces all active overlays. **Fill section with selected surface** uses Strength × Flow uniformly on the configured section and ignores radius, falloff and the Paint/Erase choice.
7. Choose **Brush action > Smooth** to soften all surface weights under the brush toward a brush-local average. The selected surface is not a smoothing destination. Release and start another stroke for another averaging pass. Fill and weathering are unavailable in Smooth; choose Paint or Erase to use them. **Path smoothing (%)** stabilizes motion and is a separate control.
8. Undo once to restore a complete stroke; redo restores it. Paint assets save automatically after strokes and surface edits, including Undo/redo. **Save painted asset** remains an immediate save action. Save the scene or prefab yourself to retain its object references; reopen and inspect the result before relying on the saved scene.

**Advanced setup** retains an optional [surface palette](surface-palettes.md), mesh section and 256/512/1024-square mask resolution. Higher resolutions retain smaller details but use more memory, save space and Undo storage. A chosen palette is copied; its original material is not edited.

Paint moves toward the selected surface and reduces competing weights. Base is the remaining weight. These are blended material weights, not an ordered layer stack. New texture paint copies use smooth blending even when their source palette uses height contrast; the original palette material remains unchanged. The copied material's Inspector still exposes Height contrast for explicit edits.

## Paint material sections

Use **Section** above the surface list to select a material section. Prepared sections show **Ready**. Selecting another ready section keeps the painting tool active and shows that section's own surfaces. Surface edits, removal, Fill, immediate saving and asset details always use the displayed section.

For **Not prepared** sections, choose **Prepare section**. This explicit action copies the primary section's named surfaces, material inputs and smooth/height blend setting into a separate saved material and initially empty mask, then resumes painting on the new section. One- and two-surface paint setups are supported too. Assets are uniquely named beside the primary paint asset; retained assets from earlier Undo operations are never overwritten. Existing UVs and earlier painted weights do not change. Selecting an unprepared section stops the brush and selects the bound scene object, keeping preparation available even after Project-asset selection. Prepare it to resume, or select a ready section and choose Start painting. Neither hovering nor automatic targeting creates assets.

**Section targeting > Automatic** chooses the nearest prepared section under the pointer when a stroke starts. An unprepared section blocks the brush; it is not skipped to find a prepared section behind it. The current stroke stays on that section until release: dragging into another section stops dabs there and resets the sampled path rather than bridging across the boundary. Start another stroke to target that section. This keeps each gesture in one mask and one Undo operation; continuous multi-mask strokes are not implemented. **Lock to section** keeps all strokes on the displayed section, with other sections still blocking painting through to it.

During an active tool session, each section remembers its explicitly chosen surface. On first automatic routing to another section, an overlay is matched by its exact name, not its channel index. Base maps to Base. If no unambiguous overlay name matches, painting is blocked with a message to select that section and choose a surface in the Workspace. The under-pointer feedback identifies the prospective section/surface or the reason painting is blocked. After an automatic stroke starts, the section selector and surface list follow its destination. Choices are session-local, not saved brush presets. Undo remaps the active choice by name; if that name cannot be resolved, Base is selected instead of another overlay.

Weathering uses the displayed prepared section and its named surfaces too. Switching sections cancels the old preview; it never redirects Apply to another mask. Brush hover alone does not change the weathering destination.

## Edit surfaces

Use **Edit** beside a surface to rename it or change its tint, Albedo, Normal, packed Mask, Height, tiling, offset and material scalars. Clearing a texture field removes that texture assignment. Mask packing is R = metallic, G = occlusion, A = smoothness; texture import settings are not changed automatically. Height textures affect the result only when Height contrast is enabled on the copied material.

**+ Add surface** adds an overlay and selects it without stopping painting. There can be up to four surfaces including Base. **Remove surface** asks for confirmation, then returns that overlay's painted weight to Base, compacts the remaining surfaces and preserves their weights and texture settings. Base cannot be removed but all its inputs can be edited. Removal and addition support complete Undo/redo. Adding a surface clears its newly activated mask channel; structural removal clears unused RGB channels. Alpha is preserved.

All these edits affect the owned material and paint asset, not the source palette. Objects sharing that paint asset also share these changes; the removal prompt explicitly identifies this consequence.

## Brush presets

**Quick preset** offers Soft brush (0.15 m, strength 0.6, capped), Hard brush (0.15 m, strength 1, constant falloff, capped), and Wash (0.5 m, strength 0.15, flow 0.5, build-up). Soft/Hard use flow 1. The selector applies a preset only when chosen; opening the workspace does not reset existing brush preferences.

**Flat brush**, **Chalk** and custom texture tips extend these presets with shape and stroke controls. Opacity/Flow are displayed as percentages. New built-in presets enable an explicit per-stroke opacity limit; existing preferences/custom presets keep legacy mixing unless changed. See [Brush tips and strokes](brush-strokes.md) for hardness, angle, roundness, spacing, smoothing and the diagnostic stroke preview.

**Save preset…** stores radius, opacity/strength, flow, stroke behavior, falloff and the tip/stroke settings as a reusable asset. Choose it in **Saved preset** to apply it, or use **Apply saved preset** to restore it after manual adjustments. Existing preset paths are rejected rather than overwritten. Curves and settings are copied so editing a brush cannot silently modify a saved preset. Presets leave the selected surface, Paint/Erase/Smooth action and vertex-specific channels/masks unchanged. They do not stop the active texture tool. Custom preset assets can also be created through **Assets > Create > SimpleBlend > Brush Preset**.

## Ownership and saving

For section-wide noise, border and UV-seam treatments, expand the compact **Weathering** panel below Brush. It uses the displayed section, whether primary or independently prepared. Preview highlights affected texels without saving them; Apply commits one undoable treatment and Cancel discards it. See [Texture weathering](texture-weathering.md) for controls, local-space units and preview limitations.

The initial `TexturePaintAsset` owns a mesh, material and mask as sub-assets in one file. Its serialized `Color32[]` is the authority for Undo and reconstruction. The linear RGBA32 mask has bilinear filtering, clamp addressing and no mipmaps. The RGB channels store overlay weights; unused channels are preserved by brush mixing. No PNG import, compression or color-space conversion occurs during a stroke.

Setup generates a unique atlas on the owned mesh using Unity's unwrap API. It stores paint coordinates in **UV4** (zero-based channel 3), leaving surface-texture UV0, static-lightmap UV2 and dynamic-lightmap UV3 intact through vertex remapping. UV4 on the owned copy is reserved for paint; source UV4 is not changed. Topology can gain seam vertices, but setup does not subdivide faces. The atlas includes two-pixel painted gutters to support bilinear sampling at chart edges.

The original mesh, material palette and vertex-paint data are not edited. Setup's Undo removes the new scene object and restores the original renderer; its saved asset remains for recovery. Existing asset paths are rejected. There is no automatic asset deletion on ordinary Undo, object removal or package removal.

Duplicating a painted object shares its paint asset and therefore its paint. There is no automatic per-instance fork in this path. Do not edit the owned mesh's topology or paint UVs after painting; there is no automatic remap. Painting is blocked when the assigned mesh, material or mask no longer matches the paint target's references. Automatic saving is debounced outside active input and scoped to queued paint assets, not every dirty project asset. Save exceptions are reported in the Console and kept queued for retry. Normal Unity Save Project also saves dirty paint assets. Scene/prefab saving remains explicit.

### Material-section ownership foundation

The lower-level editor API can prepare another section on an existing paint target with an explicit palette and a new asset path. It saves a separate material, mask and surface list, but references the exact mesh and UV4 atlas owned by the initial paint asset. Preparing another section never re-unwraps the mesh or repoints earlier mask data. Keep the initial paint asset alongside the additional section assets: it remains their mesh dependency. All section masks currently use the initial mask resolution.

`TexturePaintTarget.Paint` and `MaterialSection` retain their original primary binding. `PaintForSection(section)` returns the independently prepared asset or null for an unprepared section; reading it does not migrate or dirty old targets. Section-aware strokes validate the addressed binding, assigned renderer material, mesh, atlas section and resolution before writing. Preparation has its own scene Undo/redo; Undo unbinds the additional section and restores that material slot while retaining its saved reusable asset. Stroke Undo affects only the addressed paint asset. Duplicated targets share additional assets too.

The Workspace and Scene tool use these bindings for explicit preparation, section selection and automatic nearest-hit routing. Cached section atlases are rebuilt after Undo; undoing preparation of the active section returns the tool to its valid primary section. No assets are created merely by hovering over another section. Native visual and production-performance acceptance remain pending.

## Smooth texture weights

Smooth is brush-local weight averaging, not Gaussian image blur, smudge, iterative diffusion, selected-layer filtering or a global smoothing command. Each dab averages eligible covered texels under its shaped world-space footprint, using the stroke-start weights. It then blends each eligible destination's original weights toward that average using the existing opacity/flow/falloff and capped/build-up coverage. Repeated dabs do not feed already-smoothed pixels back into the source. Release and start another stroke to use the newly smoothed result as a fresh baseline. A moving dab can have a different local average; capped mode still retains only the strongest exposure at each texel.

Averages are separate for each section-local connected component and for the positive/negative normal-alignment groups relative to the dab. This separation applies even with Nearby surfaces and Either direction selected: both parts can be smoothed, but their colors do not contaminate one another. Visibility, depth, Face direction, connected-part scope, tip mask and falloff all filter both source averaging and destination writes. Same-facing folds in the same component may mix when they are inside the footprint and Through mode permits them; this is not a geodesic filter. UV chart placement does not define the neighborhood, so connected seamed geometry can share an average across charts.

Only covered source texels are counted, once each; gutters use their source and do not add duplicate samples. Source profile weights are quantized to positive 16-bit integers, excluding zero coverage. Integer channel sums make the result independent of grid traversal order. Each average remains a valid set of active shader weights with nonnegative implicit Base. Alpha and unused channels retain each destination texel's exact bytes. The filter is texel-density weighted, not surface-area weighted; atlas density, quantization and coincident geometry can affect its appearance. Two candidate passes avoid pairwise neighborhood queries, but native feel and production responsiveness still need observed review.

Smooth has no selected-surface destination. Automatic targeting can therefore begin on any prepared section without matching its surface names; unprepared sections and explicit locks still block the pointer, and one stroke remains in one mask/Undo operation. Selecting/editing surfaces, switching prepared sections and applying presets retain the active Smooth action. Explicit Paint/Erase selection changes the action without deactivating the tool. Old Paint/Erase state remains compatible. Internal callers opt in with `smooth: true` at Begin; combining Erase and Smooth or calling Fill on a Smooth stroke rejects before that operation writes anything. Fill is also rejected for an actively smoothing Scene tool. Weathering remains a separate backend operation and still pauses/finishes active brush strokes; the Workspace asks users to choose Paint/Erase before accessing it.

## Projection and current scope

The brush projects its footprint along the camera-facing hit normal. It accepts texels within a quarter-radius of depth. The default **With brush** face direction rejects authored normals with a dot product below 0.25 against that brush normal; the other direction choices are described below. Positions are evaluated in world space, so nonuniform object scale does not stretch the brush radius. A dab can cross UV chart seams because coverage is tested against surface positions, not a circle in UV space. A sharp change in surface direction may fall outside the depth/normal limits.

Paint checks visibility against all triangle sections of the painted mesh from the Scene camera captured at stroke start. A nearer material section blocks the pointer hit instead of allowing painting through to the configured section. Hidden shells are excluded from brush writes unless **Through nearby surfaces** is enabled. Perspective and orthographic views use their respective rays; gutters reuse their source texel's visibility. Face direction is independent of this visibility test. Camera or target-transform changes finish the stroke; press again to begin from the new view. Repeated dabs reuse cached visibility until the stroke ends.

This is geometric occlusion within the painted mesh, not a complete scene-rendering visibility test. Other scene objects do not block the brush; transparent, cutout, displaced and double-sided shader effects are not evaluated. The test treats every triangle section as an opaque, two-sided occluder. Depth comparisons allow a numerical tolerance of the larger of 0.00001 world units and one millionth of the view-ray distance; surfaces separated by less than that can be indistinguishable. **Fill** deliberately ignores camera visibility and covers the configured section.

### Through-paint reach

In the existing **Brush** panel, **Paint reach** defaults to **Visible surface**. Choose **Through nearby surfaces** to bypass per-texel mesh occlusion: nearby hidden surfaces in the captured section can receive paint or erase if the independent Face direction and Surface scope rules accept them. The panel shows a warning while this is enabled. It does not extend the brush's quarter-radius depth limit, change face direction or the footprint, or write another section's mask. Fill and weathering retain their existing section-wide behavior. Brush presets deliberately exclude this targeting preference, so choosing a brush cannot enable it unexpectedly.

Pointer hits and resampled dab positions still use the visible nearest mesh section. Through mode therefore cannot start a stroke through a nearer unprepared or locked section. It is not an all-depth ray brush or a backface mode. The Scene tool captures the local preference at stroke start; changing the control finishes the current stroke without deactivating the tool. The internal texture stroke's optional `throughPaint` argument defaults to false. Internal callers supplying no visibility query retain their previous behavior; the Scene tool always supplies its captured camera query.

### Connected-part scope

Beside **Paint reach**, **Surface scope** defaults to **Nearby surfaces**, preserving existing behavior. **Connected part only** captures the visible starting triangle's component within the current section. All brush texels, gutters, later pointer hits and resampled dabs must belong to that component until release. Moving over another part hides the brush and explains the restriction; returning to the starting part resumes without bridging the gap. Release and start a new stroke to choose another part. Changing the setting finishes the current stroke without stopping the tool. Fill and weathering remain section-wide.

Triangles connect through complete edges with exactly matching endpoint positions, even when UV charts, hard normals or vertex indices are split. A shared corner or a small positional gap does not connect parts, and zero-area triangles do not bridge them. Matching coincident full edges deliberately count as connected, including nonmanifold edges; this is not approximate welding or a semantic object-part detector. Connectivity never crosses material sections, including paths through an intervening section. It is topological scope, not geodesic brush distance: a folded connected part may still receive paint on nearby same-facing folds if Through mode allows it. Existing depth, normal and visibility rules still apply.

The atlas records the selected-section triangle owning each covered texel; gutters use their source texel's component. Components are built lazily from the atlas's local geometry and cached with that atlas. Existing atlas invalidation after Undo also invalidates connectivity. Scene raycasts use global triangle indices; the atlas translates the section offset before looking up a component. Internal stroke callers can provide `connectedTriangle` at Begin; null retains nearby-surface behavior and invalid section seeds reject before the stroke becomes active. The local `TextureConnectedOnly` preference is captured at Begin and excluded from both saved and built-in brush presets. None of this changes saved paint-asset formats or modifies mesh geometry.

### Face direction

Beside the existing targeting controls, **Face direction** defaults to **With brush**, retaining the original rule. For each dab it compares the world-space, interpolated authored mesh normal with the camera-facing brush normal. **With brush** accepts alignment at least 0.25; **Against brush** accepts alignment at most -0.25; **Either direction** accepts absolute alignment at least 0.25. All three exclude grazing normals and non-finite alignment. The brush-tip frame and nearest-hit routing remain camera-facing; neither authored mesh normals nor materials are changed.

Against brush can produce an empty stroke on an ordinary outward-facing surface: its visible normals are aligned, and opposite-facing hidden texels remain occluded unless Through is explicitly enabled. Either direction also allows painting a visible reverse-normal surface without enabling Through. The Scene ring still shows the projected footprint, not a guarantee that every enclosed texel passes all targeting rules. Depth, visibility, connected-part scope and material-section ownership apply independently. Fill and weathering remain section-wide.

Direction is captured at stroke start. Changing the control finishes the current stroke without stopping the tool. Saved and built-in brush presets leave this targeting preference alone. Invalid serialized preferences normalize to With brush; invalid internal `facing` values reject before the stroke becomes active. Internal callers omitting `facing` retain With brush behavior. Normals use the atlas's inverse-transpose transform under nonuniform and mirrored scale. This is an authored-normal rule, not triangle-winding or material backface-culling emulation; mirrored winding and shader double-sided/transparent behavior do not redefine it.

The Workspace supports readable static triangle meshes up to 100,000 vertices, multiple independently prepared material sections with one section per stroke or weathering operation, Paint/Erase/Fill and brush-local Smooth with shaped/textured tips, explicit through-paint reach, connected-part scope and authored-normal face direction. Triangle-winding culling and skinned/vertex targeting parity remain separate roadmap work. It does not yet offer continuous multi-section gestures, texture Sample, color-image decal placement, skinned texture painting, arbitrary UV selection, resolution changes after setup, per-instance asset forking, or texture-mode Shader Graph references. Vertex shortcuts, target locking, diagnostic vertex overlays and advanced vertex masks remain specific to **Vertex paint**.

Forward, deferred, depth-normal and meta passes of the hand-written Layer Blend materials share the texture-weight lookup. Existing materials default to vertex weights. Terrain materials and Shader Graph examples keep their previous contracts. This does not establish visual acceptance for every rendering feature or target platform.
